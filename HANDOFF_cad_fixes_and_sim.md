## Fusion API notes (learned the hard way)

**Running scripts:**

- Use the MCP tool `Autodesk_Fusion__fusion_mcp_execute` (featureType `script`) with a `def run(context)` entry point.
- Write results to files under the project folder and read them with `device_bash`. Calls often time out at 60 s while the script keeps running, so poll the file; don't blindly re-run.
- Pass `readOnly: true` for measurement scripts. A read-only script can't drive joints, so for poses use TemporaryBRep copies.

**Geometry edits:**

- Geometry lives in base features. Edit with `bf.startEdit()` → `bf.updateBody(oldBody, tempBody)` → `bf.finishEdit()` inside try/finally.
- Base-feature bodies revert to Steel on recompute. Set `comp.material` and `body.material` inside the edit, then check for Steel bodies afterwards.
- `OrientedBoundingBox3D.create` needs exactly perpendicular length and width directions. Orthonormalise them first.

**Joints and mass properties:**

- Proxy physical properties go stale after driving joints in the same script. For mass props use native body properties plus `occurrence.transform2`. `getXYZMomentsOfInertia` returns (xx, yy, zz, xy, yz, xz) in kg cm² with the off-diagonals already signed.
- Joint angles wrap: a limit of −55..0 with a value reading 359.6 blocks the solver.
  - To drive the blocker claws, temporarily disable the claw and blocker-servo limits, step the servo at most 4–20 deg per step (a big jump flips the four-bar to the wrong branch), then restore the limits.
  - The TVC pushrod loops follow the YAW/PITCH joints fine.
- **Leg hinge joints:** "Leg N (az …) hinge (revolute, deploys 119 deg)". Positive rotation deploys; limits 0..119 deg.
- **Keeper ring:** joint "Keeper ring (rotates -9 deg / clockwise to release)", limits −9.5..+1 deg. These are CAD limits, **not** physical stops.
- **Keeper motion link:** "Keeper servo drives ring (9 deg ring : 56 deg horn)" is a **linear approximation**. The real drive is a pin in a radial slot (see problem 4).
- For clearance and release sweeps, transform TemporaryBRep copies analytically and intersect them, instead of driving joints. That's read-only and exact.

### 1. Leg plunger shear limit

**Load path.** At 119 deg, an upward ground force on the foot folds the leg back toward stowed. The **only** thing holding it is the M6 spring plunger. The stop bar only stops over-travel.

**Measured in the CAD (leg-local az-0 frame, mm):**

- **Plunger body:** envelope Ø5 (modelled at the tap-drill size), in the +y cheek from y 16.25 outward.
  - The plunger is a generic envelope, not a chosen part. Mass override 3.0 g, VERIFY. Pin material unknown.
- **Pin:** Ø3.0, protruding from the cheek face (y 16.25) to y 13.25. Gap to the knuckle face 0.25 mm.
- **Engagement:** 2.75 mm in a Ø3.2 hole, y 12.75..16.0, in a Ø9 boss (y 8..16) at stowed (49, 14) = leg-local a 22.
- **Cheek plunger lobe:** r 5.5 around the Ø5 hole, so the ligament is 3.0 mm.
- **Stop bar:** r 3 at (56.50, −25.42).

**Lever at 119 deg, measured:**

### 2. EPS foot crush stress

- **0.085 MPa** came from the previous handoff prompt. It was an estimate, not from a datasheet.
- **"~35 N at 5 mm"** in the material spec corresponds to about 0.11 MPa.
- **"54 N design load"** is 1.5 × 36 N, a foot-load target carried over from the old crush-pad design (`research_landing_legs.md` line 28). It is **not** an EPS stress; it matching 0.11 MPa × πR² is a coincidence.
- **CAD assumption:** EPS at 20 kg/m³ (material "EPS foam ball (0.020 g/cc)", my assumption). The craft-ball grade is unknown.

EN 13163 compressive stress at 10 % strain (Stylite factsheet):

| Grade | Density | Stress at 10 % strain |
|---|---|---|
| EPS70 | 15 kg/m³ | 70 kPa |
| EPS100 | 20 kg/m³ | 100 kPa |
| EPS150 | 25 kg/m³ | 150 kPa |
| EPS200 | 30 kg/m³ | 200 kPa |

**Recommendation:** use about 0.10 MPa at 10 % with strain hardening, and a Monte Carlo spread of ±30 %. Weigh a ball: V = 8.18 cm³, so 0.12 / 0.16 / 0.20 g means 15 / 20 / 25 kg/m³. Do a quick crush test (force vs stroke) and update.

### 3. Stability without fins (no fins, flares or aft surfaces allowed)

**My sim results** (CP swept with the CG fixed at 0.227 m):

| Nose-first CP | Static margin | Good landings (100 runs) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.50 m | −3.3 cal | 12 % |
| 0.22 m | 0.2 cal | 44 % |
| 0.18 m | 0.7 cal | 53 % |
| 0.15 m | 1.1 cal | 63 % |
| 0.12 m | 1.5 cal | 65 % |
| 0.05 m | 2.4 cal | 59 % |

These runs used a 6.5 braking rate, a 0.05 deg keeper spread and a 3000 N plunger limit. The gain levels off at 1–1.5 cal.

**My targets:** 1–1.5 cal stable nose-first, and keep at least 0.5–1 cal stable tail-first with the legs out. The sim's tail-first CP of 0.32 m is a placeholder.

**Preliminary findings to verify:**

- **Nose-first CP is fixed by the nose.**
  - The nose is a 1.5:1 tangent ogive, length 119.25 mm (per BOM). Check against `vehicle.json` `contacts/profile`.
  - Barrowman puts a finless body's CP at the nose CP: 0.466 L_n = 55.6 mm from the tip, so x ≈ 0.583 m. RocketPy said 0.50.
  - For 1–1.5 cal nose-first the CG would have to sit at about 0.6–0.7 m, at or beyond the nose tip. **Moving the CG cannot reach the target.**
- **Rearranging existing parts** (battery and avionics into the nose) gains only about 4–8 mm of CG.
- **Nose ballast at x 0.60**, for each CG shift:

| CG shift | Ballast | Liftoff T/W | Landing mass | Pitch inertia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 m | 0.146 kg | 1.35 | 0.99 kg | 0.038 kg m² |
| 0.10 m | 0.344 kg | 1.14 | 1.19 kg | 0.055 kg m² |
| 0.15 m | 0.632 kg | 0.93 (can't lift off) | 1.48 kg (above the 1.33 kg hover limit) | 0.073 kg m² |

  None of these reaches positive nose-first margin.
- **Viscous crossflow divides the two directions.**
  - At larger angles, crossflow acts near the planform centroid, roughly x 0.32–0.35.
  - Nose-first is stable only if the CG is above that centroid; tail-first only if the CG is below it.
  - So **there is no CG range where both are passively stable** for a finless body. The only compromise is near-neutral both ways, with the CG close to the centroid.
- **Deployed legs are destabilising tail-first.** When falling tail-first they are at the leading end, so their drag acts ahead of the CG. The real tail-first CP is probably lower than 0.32, which means less stable. Model leg drag explicitly (`cd_leg` exists).
- **Nose shape barely matters.** A shorter or blunter nose, or a different profile, moves the nose CP by only centimetres; the nose normal-force slope stays about 2.

**Ideas to evaluate in the sim** (these are operational or configuration changes, not new aft surfaces):

- Shorten the unpowered coast (apogee or landing-ignition logic).
- Legs out at burnout, so they act like a shuttlecock skirt on the coast up. But the rocket then wants to fall nose-down, so check whether it can flip in time.
- Spin.
- Keep the CG low and live with the coast tumble.

2. Report CP vs angle of attack.

### 4. Leg keeper release margin

**Measured geometry** (leg-local, φ measured from the leg's azimuth):

- **Ring:** r 30–37.6, Z 248–251. Notch per leg at φ +4.0..+24.5 deg for r ≥ 33.5.
- **Printed arc peg:** r 36, Ø2.4, φ +1.6..+15.3 deg with a sphere-capped tip, at Z 249.5.
- **Tip-cap tab:** x 34..46, y 4.9..8.1, Z 247.75..251.25, with a Ø3.0 hole along the tangent at φ 10.4 deg, centred (35.41, 6.50, 249.5).
- **Lower-shell tab slot:** φ 5.75..13.25 at r 39.2, Z about 247–252.
- **All four legs are an identical 90 deg pattern,** so the CAD spread is 0. Real spread comes from print tolerance.

**Release angle (analytic).** The peg tip clears the tab face when 36·sin(15.3° + ψ) + 1.2 ≤ 4.9, i.e. **ψ ≈ 9.40 deg**. The sim uses 9.1.

**The drive is a pin in a slot:**

- Horn arm 6.0 mm (shaft at (0, 28), hole at (0, 34)). The slot is radial at az 90 and 2.3 mm wide. The M2 set screw is 2.0 mm, so there is 0.3 mm of lost motion: **up to 0.27 deg of ring lag**.
- **True relation:** ψ = atan(6 sinθ / (28 + 6 cosθ)). Horn 56 deg → 9.01, 62 deg → 9.75, 70 deg → 10.63, 72 deg → 10.82; maximum 12.37 deg at 102.4 deg.

**Stops:**

- There is **no physical stop at 9.5 deg**; that's only the CAD joint limit.
- The first real contact, if a leg is still stowed, is the notch end wall reaching the tab inner corner at **ψ ≈ 11.1 deg**.

**Margin at a 62 deg horn:** 9.75 − 0.27 − 9.40 ≈ **0.1 deg**, worse than the sim assumed.

**Smallest fix (CAD, needs approval):** shorten each printed peg tip from φ 15.3 to about 14.0 deg (about 0.8 mm shorter).

- Release moves to about 8.1 deg.
- With the 62 deg command the margin is about 1.4 deg, and there is still about 1.6 deg below the 11.1 deg contact.
- The peg still spans the full tab plus 0.66 mm.
- Optionally narrow the slot to 2.1 mm.

**No-CAD alternative:** command 72 deg. That gives about 1.15 deg of margin but only 0.55 deg from the tab contact.

**Verify:**

1. In Fusion, sweep the ring rotation ψ with TemporaryBRep intersections for all 4 legs. A leg counts as blocked if the cap, rotated 0.3–4 deg about its hinge, intersects the rotated ring.
2. Find the real physical ring stop (ring vs lower-shell lugs, caps and drive).
3. Check that stowed retention against vibration and acceleration is unchanged.
